  Misleading results

  1. "Use explicit disclosure: no engagement loss, no regulatory risk." The
     disclosure labels can't be checked against the posts themselves. Posts
     labelled as disclosing in hashtags contain #ad/#sponsored only 0.23% of the
     time, about the same as organic posts (0.21%). The only comparison
     available is explicit vs. implicit, and both labels are random. This
     finding can't support a compliance claim.
  2. Hidden sponsorship can't be studied. The description says sponsored posts
     can have no disclosure, but the data has zero such posts. The biggest
     transparency risk is simply not in the dataset.
  3. "Short content (<60) engages more" and the "180–360 s" pockets mix units.
     content_length means seconds for video and word count for text, and images
     have a "length" too. All four formats have the same 10–599 distribution,
     yet the bins in data.py:20 treat them as one scale.
  4. Posting-time insights ("top 1% of comments at 10h", "YouTube before noon"):
     post_date has no timezone and the audiences are spread across 8 countries.
     "10h" doesn't correspond to anyone's local time, so these can't become
     actions.
  5. "Only 1% of sponsored posts are under the threshold" and the "nano" 
     recommendation. That 1% comes from the generator: follower counts are
     spread evenly from 0 to 1M, so 90% of posts are macro or mega. "Nano is
     equivalent" also rests on the smallest group (454 posts), which has the
     least statistical power.
  6. The 10,050-follower threshold also assumes 20 interactions per 100 views.
     Real benchmarks are roughly 1–6%, so the threshold isn't credible in either
     direction.
  7. "Focus on the platforms where your target customer is." Audience country is
     spread evenly across platforms (Bilibili and RedNote have about 12% US and
     Brazil audiences), and language is unrelated to country. The dataset can't
     answer this question.
  8. Comment sentiment runs on 0–5 auto-generated sentences per post, while
     comment counts are at least 140. That sample doesn't represent the
     comments.

  Analyses not yet done that would help marketing

  1. A disclosure compliance audit by country rules (US, UK, Germany, India). It
     doesn't depend on engagement effects: 47% of sponsored posts use implicit
     disclosure and 33% disclose only inside the video. It should also check
     that the label shows up in the post text.
  2. Sponsor–niche fit: the sponsor's industry against the content category.
     Here it's evenly spread, so about two-thirds of sponsorships fall outside
     the creator's niche. With real data, this becomes a creator selection
     criterion.
  3. A check that language, audience country and platform agree, as a
     data-quality and targeting step for real data.
  4. Length analysis split by format, with seconds for video and words for text.
  5. Posting hour converted to the audience's local time before any timing
     advice.
  6. The minimum detectable effect for each subgroup (nano, small formats) shown
     next to every "equivalent" verdict.
